# Kernel-unlocking deferred defects

Running list of real defects found while executing kernel-unlocking increments that were
**deliberately not fixed** in the increment that found them, because they were out of its
scope and not caused by it.

Recording a defect here is not a plan, a diagnosis, or a numbered directive. It exists so a
known failure cannot quietly become invisible between increments.

Format: `date | defect | where | found during`

- 2026-11-09 | CONFIRMED, NOT REPAIRED (IRQ-GENERATION1 hard stop) — revoking a stale Notification capability destroys the NEWER Notification in the same slot. The broad `destroy_notification_for_revoked_cap` and the split `destroy_notification_for_revoked_object_split` discard the cap's generation, and the rank-3 bodies (`destroy_notification`, `destroy_notification_split`) tear down by index alone: routes, waiter, object, generation bump. A stale cap needs no special setup, because revoking one cap of a SIGNAL/RECEIVE pair destroys the object and leaves its partner naming `{index, old generation}`. Reproduced by `kernel::boot::tests::irq_generation1` G1, G2, G3, G5 and G6, broad and split (10 of 12 fail on `ff7cab55`; G4, valid teardown, passes). The repair is ready to design: compare the generation in the same rank-3 critical section, through one primitive shared by both paths. It is held until the lifetime question below is decided | src/kernel/boot/capability_lifecycle_state.rs, src/kernel/boot/ipc_state.rs | found during IRQ-GENERATION1
- 2026-11-09 | CONTRACT CONTRADICTION (IRQ-GENERATION1 hard stop) — the documented Notification lifetime ("single-owner: the creator mints exactly one SIGNAL + one RECEIVE cap into its own cnode; never granted cross-process; revoking ANY cap destroys the object") is not enforced. The only production creator, `provision_init_uart_irq_witness`, mints a third RECEIVE cap into init's cnode. Generic delegation accepts Notification caps. Fork inherits them (`fork_should_inherit_capability`). Ordinary IPC cap transfer carries them (`doc/CAPABILITY_MODEL.md`, Stage 188C). So a second process can hold the object, and its revoke or exit destroys the object for every holder: `kernel::boot::tests::irq_generation1_lifetime` L1–L3 pass on `ff7cab55`. Not reachable on a default image, because only the default-off UART IRQ witness creates a Notification and no syscall does. Needs an owner decision: keep destroy-on-any-revoke and correct the contract text, refuse cross-process copies, or adopt a different lifetime model | src/kernel/boot/capability_lifecycle_state.rs, src/kernel/boot/mod.rs (`provision_init_uart_irq_witness`), src/kernel/boot/thread_state.rs, src/kernel/syscall/fork_txn.rs | found during IRQ-GENERATION1
- 2026-11-09 | RESOLVED (NOTIF-LIFETIME1) — both IRQ-GENERATION1 entries above. The owner chose a capability-reference lifetime. Notification objects now live while any live capability names their exact incarnation, and one generation-checked owner destroys them at the last reference, on the broad and the split paths (`doc/IPC.md` §8.8). G1–G6 and L1–L3 pass with the new contract | src/kernel/boot/notification_lifetime.rs | resolved in NOTIF-LIFETIME1
